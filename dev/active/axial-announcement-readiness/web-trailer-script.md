# Axial web trailer: script and editing guide

Status: planning guide updated after the first complete 44-second landscape and portrait edit on September 22, 2026. The timing table below remains the original shot proposal; the actual edit is recorded in [video-production.md](video-production.md). Caden's playback review is still pending.

The finished first cut keeps 12.5 seconds of silent gameplay before “This is Axial,” then shows a height-changing 3D line, connect five, a larger board, and two/three-line objectives. The optional “Look for a line. Watch for theirs.” beat was removed, while the overall cut remains 44 seconds. Real browser gameplay was captured frame by frame and original synthetic narration/music/effects were generated. The planned overhead-follow shot and in-scene camera orbit were not captured in this cut.

Camera feasibility, overhead-follow mechanics, capture timing and sound layers are developed in [the cinematic capture plan](cinematic-capture-plan.md). Its shot options fit within this proposed structure; the script and timings below remain unchanged.

## Creative direction

Start inside a match. A threat develops, an opponent blocks it, another threat appears, and the final piece reveals the winning line. Let viewers understand the action before introducing the name. The opening should feel like a small scene with a beginning and a payoff, rather than a collection of attractive board shots.

The science-fiction feeling comes from the sound, controlled camera movement, deliberate pauses and restrained typography. Keep Axial's existing colors, shapes and interface recognizable. Use an original female synthetic voice with an even, slightly robotic delivery. The reference is the cool atmosphere of a futuristic computer speaking quietly, with enough clarity and warmth to invite someone to play.

The visual treatment should stay clean. A subtle tonal shift and soft electronic sound bed are enough to test the direction. Heavy grain, constant glitch effects and elaborate HUD graphics would make the board harder to read and compete with the game itself.

## Proposed 44-second edit

Use captions for the spoken lines. Short feature titles can occupy the same text area where they communicate the same message, avoiding two competing blocks of text. The silent opening must communicate through framing and action; captions are not a substitute for making the blocks legible.

## Full narration

> This is Axial.
>
> Connect four. In three dimensions.
>
> Look for a line. Watch for theirs.
>
> Then change the rules. Connect five.
>
> Change the size of the board.
>
> Make it two lines to win. Or three.
>
> Your move. Play Axial in your browser.

42 words across the final 31.5 seconds, with deliberate gaps. The voice should not fill every gap. The wording is intentionally simple because the opening, rule variations and article provide the depth.

If the middle feels crowded, cut **“Look for a line. Watch for theirs.”** and its separate three-second beat first. That gives a 41-second cut while preserving the opening and the requested customization examples. Do not solve crowding by making the voice unnaturally fast.

## Voice audition direction

Use an original voice, without attempting to recreate a particular actor, character or existing performance. Start with this direction for an audition:

> An adult female synthetic voice. Calm, precise and lightly detached, with a restrained science-fiction atmosphere. Narrow pitch range, clear consonants and an unhurried rhythm. Slightly robotic through timing and steadiness, while remaining intelligible and pleasant. Quiet confidence; no sales-announcer enthusiasm, exaggerated breathiness or theatrical menace. Allow real pauses between thoughts. Say “Axial” naturally as “AK-see-uhl.”

Audition the opening introduction, one rule phrase and the closing invitation together. Compare a minimally processed synthetic voice against a slightly more mechanical version. Keep the dry original so the effect can be reduced if it obscures speech on phone speakers. Any vocoder or doubling should sit subtly underneath the intelligible main voice.

Sound direction: a low pulse for the match, a brief reduction before the winning impact, then a broader electronic texture under the introduction. Avoid a busy melody under the narration. Preserve separate narration, music and game-audio tracks so the rhythm and mix remain editable.

## Opening continuity and validated move candidate

The following is an optional staging candidate, not a captured scene or evidence of optimal AI play. It was checked against the current game core with Node on September 22, 2026: each move is legal, play continues through move 14, and move 15 wins for Player 1 with one four-piece line. The check used the existing `createGame` and `applyMove` functions from `axial-web/packages/core/src/index.ts`; it did not modify game code.

Settings: Classic, default 6-height × 6-row × 7-column board, connect four, one line to win. Coordinates below are zero-based `(row, column)`. Players alternate, starting with Player 1. All pieces in this candidate land at height 0.

Pre-scene setup, moves 1–10:

```text
P1 (0,0)    P2 (2,1)
P1 (0,1)    P2 (4,2)
P1 (1,1)    P2 (5,5)
P1 (2,3)    P2 (5,6)
P1 (2,4)    P2 (3,6)
```

On-screen sequence, moves 11–15:

| Move | Placement | Meaning in the scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | P1 `(0,2)` | Three pieces along row 0 threaten a fourth at `(0,3)`. |
| 12 | P2 `(0,3)` | The first visible threat is blocked. |
| 13 | P1 `(2,2)` | Creates a fork: the row-2 connection and the depth diagonal now have winning continuations. |
| 14 | P2 `(2,5)` | Closes the row-2 threat; the diagonal is the only remaining immediate win for Player 1. |
| 15 | P1 `(3,3)` | Completes the diagonal `(0,0) → (1,1) → (2,2) → (3,3)` and wins. |

This deliberately simple sequence makes the story easy to follow. The winning diagonal travels across the bottom plane through the board's depth; it is **not** a diagonal that climbs through height. A later gameplay example should show a raised stack or height-changing line so the full 3D game is represented. If a richer, elevated opening looks substantially better, replace this candidate only after checking its complete legal move sequence and readability.

Capture each angle from the same saved/replayed match state. Keep Player 1 and Player 2 visually consistent. Do not mirror footage. Avoid crossing to the opposite side of the board during a block; move gradually around one side so the audience can keep track of the threatened space. Capture actual new moves for their landing audio, since restored snapshots do not emit fresh impact sounds.

The same check enumerated immediate winning drops for both players after each move. Player 1 has exactly one winning continuation after move 11, none after move 12, two after move 13 (`(2,5)` and `(3,3)`), and only the final diagonal at `(3,3)` after move 14. Player 2 also develops a next-turn threat at `(1,4)` after move 14, but Player 1 wins first. This establishes the intended threat, block, fork, block and payoff without an earlier unblocked winning move.

The two blocks should be readable without a fabricated danger line or extra in-game effect. First try camera framing and a short hold on the empty destination. If that still does not communicate the threat, improve the sequence or simplify to one clearly understood block rather than adding more cuts.

## Capture checklist for the edit

- Opening match: clean setup, moves 11–15, winning hold, and a few seconds of handles around each shot. Record a readable wider continuity take before close-ups.
- Basic gameplay: an uncomplicated drop, board orbit, raised stack and a clear line involving height. Validate any staged finish before capture.
- Rule examples: connect-five finish; two-line and three-line examples with actual line progress/result UI. The final move sequences have now been selected, captured, and checked against the shared game core; see `axial-video/scripts/capture-features.mjs` and [video-production.md](video-production.md).
- Settings: clean, short selections for line length, line goal and board size. Each changed setting leads into a separate example match.
- End card source: quiet board composition with room for the title and URL.
- Capture portrait-friendly versions of the important angles. Keep the threatened destination, blocking piece and full winning line within the portrait framing; do not rely on a center crop of the landscape edit.

## First editing checkpoint

Build a rough 12–16-second opening plus title reveal first, in landscape and portrait. Temporary timing markers are sufficient until the footage and voice auditions exist. Review whether the threats and final win are understandable with the sound off, then assess tension and release with audio. Lock that rhythm before recording every customization shot.

The first proof should answer three questions: does the opening make sense, does the science-fiction tone still feel like Axial, and does the voice invite someone to play? The full edit can then follow this guide, with timing adjusted to the footage rather than forcing every clip into its provisional slot.
